# reader-side paper audit

## 1. reader conclusion
- scope status: in scope
- evidence viability: non-auditable
- viability flags: critical-method-omission + missing-comparator + selective-success-only + promotional-asymmetry
- paper type: promotional technical brief

The brief makes comparative performance claims about a named product, but it does not disclose the sample definition, test protocol, baseline identity, selection rule for shown demonstrations, or the underlying measurements needed to reconstruct the comparison. The central performance claim therefore cannot be audited from the available material. Product specifications and described use cases may still be useful as documentation, but they should not be converted into evidence of superiority.

## 2. core claims

not applicable — the central comparative claims are non-auditable because the disclosed material omits decision-critical methods, comparator details, and the denominator behind selected success cases

## 3. evidence and support

not applicable — no reconstructable paper-local evidence chain is exposed for the central superiority claims

## 4. what is usable

### usable results
No decision-critical comparative result is usable as evidence from the disclosed material.

### usable methods or design
The brief names a nominal application workflow, but not enough detail is provided to reconstruct or evaluate the test.

### usable materials or documentation
Product features, claimed operating range, terminology, and intended use cases are usable as vendor documentation.

## 5. what to downweight

### worth noticing but should be downweighted
The shown successful demonstrations may establish that the product can operate in selected examples.

### should be treated cautiously or ignored
Claims of superior accuracy, efficiency, reliability, or general performance should be ignored until the baseline, test conditions, complete result set, and selection rule are disclosed.

## 7. uncertainty and follow-up

A meaningful audit would require the test protocol, sample/denominator, named baseline, complete result distribution, failure cases, and selection rule for the displayed examples.
